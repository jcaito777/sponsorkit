Where to Find Sponsors

Not sure who to ask? Start here. These are the businesses most likely to say yes. Enter your city or zip below and every card gets a button that opens a live Google Maps search right in your area.

🔫
Gun Shops & Firearm Dealers
Your members are their customers. Gun shops love being associated with organized hunting clubs — it builds loyalty and keeps their name in front of serious buyers year-round.
How to approach: Walk in and ask for the owner or manager. Introduce yourself as the chapter president. A face-to-face ask closes far better than a cold email.
Find gun shops near me
🦌
Taxidermists
Taxidermists depend almost entirely on hunters for their business. Sponsoring your banquet puts their name in front of dozens of potential clients in one room.
How to approach: Call ahead to make sure they're not swamped in season. Offer a table mention and logo placement — many will donate a mount or gift certificate as in-kind.
Find taxidermists near me
🏕️
Outfitters & Guide Services
Banquets are prime booking season for outfitters. A sponsored table or raffle item gets them in front of people actively planning hunts — it's direct marketing for them.
How to approach: Email or call with a specific ask — a hunt package for the live auction works extremely well and costs them relatively little upfront.
Find outfitters near me
🛻
Truck & ATV Dealerships
Hunters buy trucks and ATVs. Dealerships have marketing budgets specifically for community sponsorships. A banquet logo placement and verbal mention reaches exactly their target buyer.
How to approach: Ask for the fleet or commercial sales manager — they often control the community sponsorship budget. A $500–$1,000 ask is very reasonable for a dealership.
Find dealerships near me
🎯
Sporting Goods & Archery Shops
Local sporting goods stores live or die by their community relationships. Sponsoring your event is one of the best ways they can stay top-of-mind with dedicated hunters.
How to approach: Ask about an in-kind donation (gear, gift card) in addition to or instead of cash. Many shops prefer this and it makes a great raffle item.
Find sporting goods shops near me
🍺
Local Restaurants & Breweries
Restaurants want group business and repeat customers. Sponsoring your banquet — or catering part of it — gets them in front of a room full of locals who dine out regularly.
How to approach: Talk to the owner, not a manager. Pitch a "presenting sponsor of the dinner" or "official drink sponsor" title — it feels bigger than just a logo.
Find restaurants near me
🏨
Hotels & Event Venues
If your banquet is at a hotel, they're already invested. Even if it's not, local hotels want hunting-season bookings and out-of-town guest referrals from your members.
How to approach: Talk to the sales or events coordinator. Offer to promote them as the "official lodging partner" for your chapter's events throughout the year.
Find hotels & venues near me
🌾
Farm & Ranch Supply Stores
Many of your members own land, run livestock, or manage properties. Farm supply stores (local or regional) see your chapter as a direct line to their core customer base.
How to approach: Bring a one-page sponsorship packet. These businesses often have an annual community giving budget — ask early, ideally 3–4 months before your event.
Find farm & ranch stores near me
🏥
Local Doctors, Dentists & Attorneys
Professionals with discretionary income who want community visibility. Many are hunters themselves. A $250–$500 table sponsorship is easy for them and feels personal.
How to approach: Personal connection is key here. If a member goes to a local dentist or knows an attorney, that warm intro is worth 10 cold calls. Ask your members to make referrals.
Find local professionals near me
🔧
Gunsmiths & Custom Shops
Specialized craftspeople who rely on referrals from the hunting community. Being associated with your chapter gives them credibility and word-of-mouth they can't buy elsewhere.
How to approach: Offer a "featured artisan" mention in the program and on social media. Ask if they'd donate a service (cleaning, scope mounting) as a raffle item.
Find gunsmiths near me
🌲
Land Management & Real Estate
Land brokers, timber companies, and hunting land specialists want direct access to buyers and landowners in the hunting community. Your banquet puts them in the room.
How to approach: Pitch a "Land & Conservation Sponsor" title that aligns with your chapter's mission. Frame it as a partnership, not just advertising.
Find land brokers near me
📸
Photographers & Videographers
Local photographers who shoot outdoors, wildlife, or events want exposure in the hunting community. Many will sponsor in exchange for being the official event photographer.
How to approach: Offer an in-kind trade — they cover the event for free, you promote them as the "official chapter photographer" all year on social media.
Find photographers near me

Field Notes
01
Lead with your audience. Before listing a single benefit, tell sponsors who will be in the room. Attendance numbers and demographics belong at the top of your deck — not your mission statement.
02
Drop the round numbers. Price tiers at $1,500, $3,500, and $7,500 instead of $1,000, $3,000, and $5,000. Intentional pricing signals intentional packaging.
03
Name your tiers after things that mean something. "Field Sponsor" tells a story. "Bronze Sponsor" tells nothing. The right name does half the selling before anyone reads the benefits.
04
Start Presenting Sponsor conversations early. The best sponsorships take months to develop. If you're reaching out just weeks before your event, you're getting whatever's left in their budget.
05
Build renewal in from day one. After the event, send every sponsor a one-page recap — attendance, dollars raised, where their logo appeared — with an early-bird renewal offer for next year.
06
Your top-tier ask should never be an email. That conversation belongs on the phone or in person, coming from the chapter president directly. A PDF alone won't close it.
